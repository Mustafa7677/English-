Consequences of cheating

Cheating means acting dishonestly to gain an unfair advantage. It may look like an easy shortcut, but it usually costs much more than it gives. The table below shows its consequences in ten areas of life.

Table 1. Consequences of cheating

Short-term and long-term effects, by area of life

Area Example of cheating Short-term consequence Long-term consequence Seriousness
01School Copying homework or using notes in a test Zero mark, detention, call to parents Gaps in knowledge, poor results in later exams Medium
02University Plagiarism, buying essays, cheating in exams Failing the course, disciplinary hearing Suspension or expulsion, record on transcript High
03Learning Letting others do your work Feeling of an easy success Lack of real skills and knowledge Medium
04Reputation Being caught cheating by teachers or classmates Embarrassment and shame Loss of trust and respect from others High
05Career Fake diplomas, lying on a CV Being rejected or fired Damaged career, difficulty finding a job High
06Law Exam fraud, forging documents Fines, investigation Criminal record, prison in serious cases High
07Relationships Being unfaithful to a partner Arguments, broken trust Break-up or divorce, emotional pain High
08Mental health Hiding dishonest actions Stress, guilt, fear of being caught Anxiety, low self-esteem Medium
09Sports and games Doping, using cheats in online games Disqualification, account ban Lost titles and medals, lifetime ban High
10Society Widespread cheating in education and work Unfair results for honest people Less qualified professionals, lower public trust Low

Seriousness for “Society” is low for each person, but high for the community as a whole.

Conclusion
Cheating may bring a quick result, but its consequences are much bigger than the benefit. It harms our education, career, relationships and self-respect. Honesty and hard work are the only way to real success.
